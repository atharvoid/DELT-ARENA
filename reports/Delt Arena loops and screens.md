# Delt Arena: five loops, one coach, measured screens

Delt Arena should be built as five loops (plan, lift, ride the machine, read, adjust) served by twenty-one screens that share one visual rulebook, with exactly one Coach screen where an AI proposes changes to training and the user answers with taps. The research supports the parts that matter most for trust: a deterministic rule engine for every number, an AI layer that only explains and asks, clear dismiss, correct and "why" controls on every card ([Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)), a rest timer that lives in an Android Live Update ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)), muscle volume counted in fractional sets ([SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)), and machine cardio logged through Bluetooth FTMS where it exists, a console photo where it does not, and a short manual form as the floor. The live set screen has two modes: by default a tap anywhere logs the set the app predicts, a press and hold raises a thumb keypad, and two large halves appear when the app is unsure; an optional voice mode lets the user say the set and watch an agent perform it on screen, with one-tap undo on every action. The proposed visual language is called Tolerance. It borrows from metrology and engineering drawing: every number sits on a ruled scale next to its evidence-based target band, the coach writes in the margin like an editor, and only one element per screen carries the action colour. It is light-first and ink-based, which separates it from the dark neon look of most gym apps and from the earlier Pit Wall, Thermal and Now Lifting concepts. The weak points are real and listed at the end: most competitor flows, the Material 3 Expressive spec, the FTMS byte layouts, the Concept2 PM5 interface, the BLE heart-rate profile and the licence terms of the hosted GIF libraries could not be read from the research sandbox, so those parts of the plan rest on design judgement and must be checked before build.

Throughout this report, a claim with a link comes from the research notes. Anything without a link is design judgement, and the final section lists which judgements most need testing.

## The user has four motives, so the app has five loops

A lifter opens a gym app for one of four reasons, and each reason has a different tolerance for effort. Between sets, with chalk on their hands and 60 to 180 seconds of rest, they want to record what just happened and see what comes next; any extra tap is a cost paid dozens of times per session. On a cardio machine they want the machine's numbers kept without typing them. On the sofa on Sunday they want to know whether the week worked. When something goes wrong (pain, a stall, a missed fortnight) they want a decision made for them that they can trust. Planning and onboarding are the fifth, rarer motive: setting the whole thing up once.

| Loop | Job it does | When it runs | Effort budget | Main screens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan | Turn goal, body, schedule, equipment and injuries into a program the user believes in | First open, then rarely | 2 minutes once | Setup, Plan, Program editor |
| Lift | Log each set as fast as it is performed and tell the user what is next | 3 to 6 times a week, 15 to 30 sets each | One tap for a set done as planned | Today, Session, Swap sheet, Exercise sheet, Summary |
| Machine cardio | Capture time, work and heart rate from a treadmill, stair climber, rower, bike, elliptical or ski erg | 2 to 5 times a week | Zero typing when the machine connects; under 20 seconds when it does not | Cardio start, Cardio live, Console capture, Summary |
| Read | Show whether training is working, per lift, per muscle, per machine and for the body | Weekly, plus a glance after each session | Answer in one screen, detail one tap away | Week, Lift, Muscles, Cardio stats, Body, Recovery, History |
| Adjust | Turn the data into specific changes and let the user approve, edit or refuse them | After sessions, at week end, and on events (pain, stall, gap) | One tap to accept, one to undo | Coach |

The loops feed each other in one direction. Lift and Cardio produce records; Read turns records into measures; Adjust turns measures into proposals; accepted proposals change the Plan; the Plan sets the next Lift. The Coach screen sits only on the Adjust loop. Everywhere else the app behaves as a fast tool, and the coach's presence on those screens is limited to one-line margin notes that point back to the Coach screen.

The competitor research supports this split without giving step counts. Strength loggers are praised for fast, quiet logging (Strong is described as "minimal" and suited to lifters who already have a program) ([TechBloat summary](https://www.techbloat.com/10-lifting-apps-compared-for-easier-progressive-overload.html)), while the AI planner Fitbod draws complaints about repetitive workouts, crashes and support problems even as it is praised for adapting to feedback and equipment ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/fitbod-app-review-2026-honest-take-after-real-testing-45d5f07a1b), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fitbod.me)). The reading here is that reliable logging is the price of entry and the coach earns its place only on top of it. A third-party dashboard, LiftShift, exists just to turn Hevy and Strong exports into analytics ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/liftshift-app/liftshift)), which suggests the Read loop is underserved. No source could be reached that describes how any competitor handles gym machine cardio, so that loop has no benchmark at all.

## Tolerance: a measuring-instrument design language for every screen

### Why this direction

The earlier concepts each picked one metaphor (race radio, thermal camera, music player) and stretched it across the app. Each was strong on one screen and forced on others. Tolerance starts from what the product actually does: it compares measured doses against evidence-based ranges. Weekly sets per muscle have a range, reps have a target range, rest has a duration, bodyweight change has a rate, cardio minutes have a guideline. An engineering tolerance is exactly that, a nominal value with an allowed band. So the visual language is a precision instrument: ruled scales, tick marks, bands, stamps and margin annotations, printed in ink on a warm paper surface, with one signal colour. It reads as futuristic in the way a well-made measuring tool does, through restraint and exactness, and it is practical because almost every element is a number placed against a reference.

The direction is light-first. Most fitness apps default to near-black with neon accents; a gym floor is usually brightly lit, and dark-on-light text with a single saturated accent reads well at arm's length. A dark variant (called Darkroom) uses the same roles for night use and for users who set the system to dark.

### Principles

The rules below apply to every screen with no exceptions, which is the consistency the user asked for.

1. One signal per screen. Exactly one element uses the Signal colour: the primary action. If a screen seems to need two, one of them is not primary. On the live set screen the whole centre is the primary action, so Signal appears there as a thick rule under the predicted reps.
2. Every number sits on its scale. Where an evidence-based or user-set reference exists (target reps, weekly set band, rest time, rate of bodyweight change), the number is drawn on a ruled scale with the band shaded. A number with no reference is shown plain and smaller.
3. The coach writes in the margin. Anything produced by the AI layer is set in the Margin style (indigo rule on the left, monospaced text, a small "AI" tag) so that machine-written text is never confused with the user's own data. Proposals that change anything appear only on the Coach screen.
4. Done things are stamped. A logged set, a completed session or an applied change turns into a square, filled, slightly heavier row. Shape tells state: rounded means waiting for you, square means recorded.
5. Colour is never the only code. Under-band and over-band use colour plus a marker shape (an open triangle pointing up for under, a filled triangle pointing down for over) and the word.
6. Thumb zone for action, top zone for reading. The primary action always sits in the bottom third, reachable one-handed; reading content flows from the top.

The contrast figures were computed for this report against the WCAG formula; the research could not confirm Android's own numeric contrast thresholds from a fetched source, so the plan targets WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 for text) because the European Accessibility Act benchmark, EN 301 549, is based on WCAG 2.1 AA and a move to 2.2 is reported ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eu-accessibility-requirements-and-eaa-compliance/)). Signal and Alert are close in hue for some colour-vision types, which is why Alert never appears without its stop icon and text, and Signal never appears on anything except the primary button and the live rest indicator.

The app does not use Android dynamic colour (wallpaper-derived palettes) for its roles, because the Band, Under and Over meanings must stay fixed. It can tint the Paper surface slightly from the wallpaper if testing shows users want it.

### Type

Numbers do most of the work, so the type system is built around them. Roboto Flex, a variable font that Google cites as the third-party equivalent for Expressive's variable-axis type ([Android Developers, Wear M3 Expressive](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/apply)), sets all numerals with tabular figures so columns of weights align. Its width axis is used as feedback: a number being edited widens slightly, and a stamped number returns to normal width. Text labels use Roboto Flex at normal width. The Margin style uses a monospaced face (Roboto Mono or JetBrains Mono) so coach text is recognisable at a glance and reads like an annotation. The scale is short: Display numerals for the current set's weight and reps (sized so they read from a bench, around 64 to 96sp), Title for screen and exercise names, Body for content, Label for units and ticks. All sizes are in sp and the layouts are tested at 200% font scale; nothing truncates a weight.

### Shape

Data containers are rectangles with a 4dp corner. Scales are drawn as open rules without boxes. The Signal action is the only full pill on a screen, and it morphs to a square stamp when its action completes (for example "Log 82.5 × 6" becomes a square "Logged" for 600ms, then the next set's pill appears). Material 3 Expressive introduces a shape library with built-in shape morphing ([codelab listing via search](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/mdc-102-objc)); Tolerance uses only this one morph so that the morph always means the same thing.

### Motion

Motion follows Expressive's spring physics in spirit, but Flutter does not ship Expressive in its SDK. The Flutter tracking issue shows maintainers paused the work in 2025 and moved Material into a separate package where Expressive is expected to land ([flutter/flutter#168813](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/168813)); community packages such as `material_3_expressive` exist but require a replacement `material_ui` package ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/material_3_expressive)). The plan is to define springs as design tokens (one "snappy" spring for stamps and toggles, one "settle" spring for sheets and page changes) in the app's own design-system layer, so a later switch to official widgets is cheap. Every interaction gets at most one motion. Numbers roll when they change. Sheets rise. Nothing loops or idles in place, except the rest indicator's draining scale. When the system's remove-animations setting is on, springs become instant changes with a 100ms crossfade, and exercise demonstrations show their start and end frames instead of playing (Flutter exposes this setting to apps; the exact API was not verified in the research).

### Haptics

Three haptic words exist and nothing else vibrates. Detent is a light tick for every increment on a ruler input (each 2.5kg or 1 rep), so weight can be set by feel. Stamp is a firm single pulse when a set, session or change is recorded, which also confirms a whole-screen tap without looking. Bell is a double pulse when rest ends, because the phone is often in a pocket or on the floor. Safety cards do not vibrate; they rely on content. Android's haptics APIs could not be fetched during research, so the exact constants are left to the build team.

### Signature components

Five components carry the language and appear on every relevant screen. The Scale shows a value on ruled ticks with its band. The Ruler input is a horizontal draggable scale with detents for weight and time, always paired with minus and plus buttons, and is used in editors outside the live session. The Thumb keypad is the live session's correction tool: a sheet of large digit keys that rises from the bottom edge under the thumb. Neither is a text field, and the Coach screen never shows either. The Stamp row is a logged set or record. The Margin note is a single line of coach text with the AI tag that opens the Coach screen. The Body plate is a line-engraved front and back figure in the style of a technical illustration, where each muscle region is filled with hatching whose density shows weekly fractional sets against the band: open outline for none, sparse hatching under band, cross-hatching in band, solid fill with the Over marker above band. Hatching works without colour, prints well, and is distinct from Thermal's heat gradient.

The bottom bar has four destinations: Today, Coach, Stats and Plan. Session and Cardio live are full-screen modes launched from Today, the widget, or the Live Update, and they hide the bar so the logging surface is uncluttered. Settings and History open from Today's top bar. The Coach destination shows a count badge only when a card needs the user's answer; informational cards never raise a badge.

### The live set screen: predictive tap by default, voice when switched on

The Session screen carries most of the app's daily use, so it gets the strictest layout rule in the app. The centre shows only the current set. Everything else lives in two slim strips. The top bar (56dp) holds the exercise name, the set count ("Set 3 of 4", or "A1 of 2" in a superset) and the rest time when rest is running; tapping the exercise name opens the Exercise sheet with its demonstration. The bottom tray (64dp, above the gesture area) holds five keys: Swap, Note, Pain, Plates and the mode switch (Tap or Voice). Logged sets appear as a slim strip of stamps directly under the top bar ("80 × 8 · 82.5 × 6"), so the user can see what they have done without opening anything. The system gesture areas at the edges are excluded from every touch target, as Android's edge-to-edge guidance asks ([System bars](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/foundations/system-bars)).

The centre shows the app's prediction for the set the user is about to do, in Display numerals large enough to read from a bench: weight on top ("82.5 kg"), reps below at the same size ("6"), and a smaller line for effort ("2 in reserve"). Both modes use the same prediction and the same layout. Tap mode is the default; Voice mode is a switch the user turns on in the tray, and the choice is remembered per user.

#### Tap mode: nothing happens until the user taps

In tap mode the app takes no action on its own. The whole centre, everything between the top bar and the bottom tray, is a single touch surface, so the user never hunts for a small button with chalky or shaking hands.

| Gesture | Result | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| Tap anywhere in the centre | Logs exactly the shown weight, reps and effort; rest starts at once | Stamp haptic; the numbers morph into a square stamp that slides into the strip |
| Press and hold anywhere in the centre (about 400ms) | Raises the Thumb keypad from the bottom edge, prefilled with the prediction | Detent haptic as the sheet starts to rise |
| Tap one half (split mode) | Logs that half's outcome | Stamp haptic; the other half fades |
| Tap the Undo chip that appears for 8 seconds after a log | Removes the last set and restores the prediction | Short single pulse |
| A second tap within 3 seconds of a log | Ignored; shows "Already logged" with Undo | None |

The Thumb keypad covers the bottom 55% of the screen so every key is in thumb reach. Its top row shows the reps already filled with the guess, so a set of 5 instead of 6 is one press on "5" and one on Log. The weight row has minus and plus keys that move by the user's smallest available increment (from their plates or dumbbell rack) and shows the per-side plates for the new weight. Effort chips (0, 1, 2, 3, 4+ reps in reserve) and set type (normal, warm-up, drop, to failure) sit above Log, which is the only Signal key on the keypad, at the bottom right (bottom left for left-handed users).

When the app is unsure, the centre splits into two halves showing the two likeliest outcomes, for example "6 reps" and "5 reps" at the same weight. The halves are vertical (left and right) so that both reach down towards the tray and stay reachable one-handed; the more likely outcome sits on the dominant-hand side. One tap on either half logs it, and press and hold still raises the keypad if neither is right. Split mode never shows more than two options, and the halves are labelled "likely" and "possible" in words, because a percentage adds reading without changing the choice.

#### Voice mode: one press, then the agent acts where the user can see it

In voice mode a mic key takes the middle of the bottom tray (Swap, Note, Mic, Pain, Plates) and the mode switch moves to the right end of the top bar. One press starts listening; there is no press-and-hold and no wake word. Listening stops after about 1.5 seconds of silence or a second press. The whole-screen tap and the keypad keep working in voice mode, so the user can mix inputs.

What the user says is shown at the top of the centre as a Margin-style caption ("Heard: 82.5 for six, two left"). The agent then performs the action on the visible screen, one step at a time, at a pace slow enough to watch (about 250ms per step). For that phrase, the keypad rises, the weight changes to 82.5, the reps to 6, the effort chip to 2, and Log is pressed and stamped. The user verifies by watching, the same way they would watch a training partner write in their log. Every action the agent performs leaves an Undo chip in the stamp strip, and a session-level list (opened from the stamp strip) shows every voice action with its own Undo.

| Phrase type | Example | What the agent does on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Log a set | "82.5 for six, two left"; "same again"; "six" | Fills weight, reps, effort as heard (missing parts default to the prediction) and logs |
| Correct the last set | "that was five" | Opens the last stamp, changes reps, re-stamps |
| Swap | "swap to dumbbells"; "swap to machine press" | Opens Swap, highlights the alternative that matches the equipment and primary muscle, selects it |
| Rest | "thirty more seconds"; "skip rest" | Presses +15s twice, or Skip |
| Note | "note: grip slipped on rep five" | Opens Note and inserts the transcript as the note |
| Plates | "plates" | Opens Plates for the current weight |
| Pain | "left shoulder hurts" | Opens the Coach screen's Pain check with the left shoulder already marked, then stops: the remaining pain questions are answered by tap |
| Undo | "undo" | Reverses the agent's last action |

The agent acts on its own only inside a closed list of session actions: log, correct, swap within the library, rest changes, notes, plates, undo, and opening the pain flow. It never changes the program, never answers a pain question on the user's behalf, and never logs a value it is unsure of. If the transcript is low confidence, or a number is far from the prediction (reps more than 3 away, or weight more than 10% away), the agent fills the keypad with what it heard, marks the doubtful field, and waits for a tap on Log. This keeps the "watch it act" model safe: a misheard "sixty" for "sixteen" stops at the keypad.

Parsing runs in two layers. A strict on-device grammar handles the set-logging phrases (numbers, units, "for", "reps", "left" or "in reserve", "same again", "undo"), which cover most of what anyone says between sets and need no network. Phrases outside the grammar go to a small intent classifier that maps them to the action list above using the exercise library's names and equipment tags. An LLM fallback, used only when online and only for unmatched phrases, may return one of the same actions and nothing else.

#### Speech recognition, permissions and privacy

The research notes contain nothing on speech recognition, so this subsection rests on general platform knowledge that the build team must verify. Android's SpeechRecognizer API can run on the device; from Android 12 an on-device recognizer can be requested directly, and later versions let an app check whether an offline model for a language is installed and trigger its download. Offline availability varies by device maker and language, so the app checks at the moment voice mode is switched on and, if no on-device model exists, either offers the download or tells the user that voice needs a connection, with a setting to allow online recognition. A bundled open-source model (Vosk or a small Whisper build) is the fallback for full offline control at the cost of tens of megabytes; the size and licence must be checked. No source was found on recognition accuracy in gym noise. The constrained number grammar, short push-to-talk windows and support for Bluetooth earbud microphones are the mitigations, and accuracy must be measured on real recordings from busy gyms before voice mode ships beyond a beta label.

RECORD_AUDIO is a runtime permission, requested only when the user first turns voice mode on, after a one-screen explanation ("Delt Arena listens only after you press the mic, and the audio stays on your phone"). If the user declines, the switch returns to Tap and nothing else changes. The app never listens in the background, never uses a wake word and never records while the screen is off, so it needs no microphone foreground service. Audio is processed in memory and discarded; only the transcript of the phrase is kept, as part of the session's action list, and the user can delete it. If online recognition is enabled, the privacy policy and Google Play's data safety form must say so, and the Health apps declaration already required for Health Connect use covers the health context ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en)).

#### How the prediction is made

The prediction is computed by the rule engine before the set begins, from four inputs: the program's prescription for this set (load, rep range, target reps in reserve), what the user did on the same exercise last time, what they have done earlier today, and how long they actually rested. The steps below are design judgement; the research found no published method for within-session rep prediction.

Weight comes from the program after auto-progression, rounded to the user's equipment, and stays the same across working sets unless the program prescribes back-off sets. For the first working set, the predicted reps are last session's first-set reps at this load. If the load went up since last time, the engine inverts the Epley formula, reps = 30 × (e1RM ÷ weight − 1), subtracts the target reps in reserve, and caps the result at the top of the rep range ([Wikipedia: 1RM](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/1RM)). Epley is reliable only for sets of roughly 2 to 10 reps ([Topend Sports](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/calculators/1repmax.htm)), so outside that range the engine falls back to last session's reps.

For later sets, predicted reps = previous set's reps − d, where d is this user's typical drop-off for this exercise at this effort. The engine learns d as the median rep drop between consecutive working sets at constant load, starting from a prior of 1 rep per set for compound lifts and 0.5 for isolation lifts until it has five sessions of data. If the user rested less than 70% of the programmed rest, d grows by half a rep. If today's earlier sets beat the prediction, the next prediction shifts up by the same amount.

Confidence comes from the spread of the user's past prediction errors on this exercise. The engine turns the prediction into a probability for each whole rep count. If the most likely count has at least 60% probability, the screen shows a single prediction. If not, but the two likeliest counts together reach 75%, the screen splits. Below that, it shows the single most likely count and raises the keypad on a shorter hold. A new exercise with no history shows the program target, labelled "target" instead of "predicted". Every logged set stores its prediction error, so the spread narrows and split mode becomes rarer as the user trains.

Effort is predicted as the program's target and is not asked on every set, because asking doubles the taps. After each exercise's last set, the rest screen shows effort chips in its lower half ("How many more could you have done? 0 · 1 · 2 · 3 · 4+"); the user can tap one, say it in voice mode, or ignore it, in which case the target is assumed. These answers drive load progression, which is reasonable because self-reported reps in reserve is usually within about one rep of the truth ([Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve)).

#### Rest, preloading and the Live Update

Rest starts automatically on every log, with its length set by the program for that exercise. The centre becomes a draining Scale with the time left in large numerals, with +15s and Skip as two clear keys, and the next set already loaded underneath: the same exercise's next set, the superset partner, or the next exercise with its setup ("Incline dumbbell press · 30 kg · bench at 30°"). When rest ends, a Bell haptic and optional tone play, and the centre switches to the prediction for the preloaded set. A tap during rest logs the next set early and restarts the clock, so the timer never blocks the user.

The same rest timer runs in an Android Live Update: a promoted ongoing notification with a Chronometer countdown in the status-bar chip, ProgressStyle segments for sets done, and +15s and Skip actions ([Live Updates](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)). Hevy offers the same +15 seconds and skip controls in its Android widget ([Hevy help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35649846517399)). A foreground service owns the countdown so it fires on time with the screen off.

Supersets need no extra taps. Logging A1 loads A2 immediately with no rest; logging A2 starts the rest and preloads A1's next set. Drop sets log as child rows of their parent set and do not start rest. Warm-up sets for the first compound lift are generated with plate breakdowns and shown as lighter numerals labelled "Warm-up 2 of 3"; they log like any other set. Strong charges for its warm-up and plate calculators ([AI Tools Bakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/strong-app-review/)), so including both free is a cheap advantage.

#### Accessibility in both modes

A screen reader intercepts taps, so the centre is built as an explicit accessibility object. It is one semantics node whose label reads the whole set ("Bench press, set 3 of 4: 82.5 kilos, 6 reps, 2 in reserve") and whose action is "log set", so TalkBack's double tap logs it. Press and hold maps to TalkBack's double-tap-and-hold and is also a custom action, "Change reps or weight", alongside "Undo last set". In split mode each half is its own node ("Option 1, likely: 6 reps"). The tray keys are ordinary labelled buttons, so Swap, Note, Pain, Plates and the mode switch need no custom actions. Switch Access and Voice Access reach everything through the same nodes and actions. Android's guidance asks for exactly this: expose gesture-only interactions as custom actions, label long-press, and give custom components a role and state ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles)). Rest time is a live region announced at 30 seconds, 10 seconds and at the end.

Voice mode has its own needs. Every agent step is announced to TalkBack as it happens ("Reps set to 6. Set logged.") so a blind user hears the same verification a sighted user sees. While the app is listening, TalkBack's own speech is paused so the recogniser does not hear the phone. The caption of what was heard stays visible until the next action, which helps users who are deaf or hard of hearing confirm that the app understood. Users who already control their phone with Android Voice Access can keep using it in tap mode; the in-app mic is a separate, narrower tool for gym phrases.

Two settings protect users for whom a whole-screen tap is risky. "Hold to log" turns the tap into a short hold, for users with tremor or who keep the phone in a loose pocket. "Button mode" replaces the centre surface with a large Signal button above the tray. The phone also ignores palm-sized contacts and touches while the proximity sensor is covered, so a phone carried between stations does not log sets by itself.

## The Coach screen: proposals the user answers with taps

### What the Coach screen does

The Coach screen is the only place where the AI proposes changes to the user's training. It has no text field and no microphone. Everything the coach needs to know it either reads from logged data or asks through tap questions. (The live set screen's optional voice mode, described above, is a separate input tool limited to session actions; it can open a pain report on the Coach screen but cannot change the program.) This departs from the norm: Whoop Coach is opened from the home screen and the user types a question ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/press-center/whoop-unveils-the-new-whoop-coach-powered-by-openai/)), and Oura Advisor is conversational ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-advisor)). The closest precedents are Strava Athlete Intelligence, which pushes per-activity insights without chat ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/i-used-stravas-new-athlete-intelligence-ai-feature-for-a-week-heres-what-happened)), and Fitbod's recovery view, where users override the app's muscle state with taps and the app "learns from your edits" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id1041517543?l=en-au)). No tap-only AI coach was found in the market, so the design is a synthesis.

### Why the Coach screen stays tap-only when voice mode is on

The user's voice-mode switch could extend to the Coach screen, and the plan keeps it off there. On the live screen, voice saves real effort: hands are busy, the phrases are short and predictable, and every action is small and visible. On the Coach screen none of that holds. The user is resting or at home, the answers are already one-tap chips, and the decisions (program changes, pain severity, red flags) carry more risk if misheard. Free speech about pain also invites the user to describe symptoms in medical terms, which pulls the app towards the diagnosis territory the FDA wellness framing and Google Play's health policy steer away from ([FDA](https://fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices), [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en)). Structured chips keep pain data clean enough for the rules to act on. Voice can start a pain report from the live screen, because speed matters mid-set, and the questions that follow are answered by tap. If testing shows users in voice mode expect to answer Coach chips by speaking the chip's label, that narrow extension (say the label, the chip highlights, the user taps to confirm) is the one to try first.

### Who computes what

A deterministic rule engine computes every number the coach proposes: next loads, weekly sets per muscle, plateau detection, deload timing, warnings, cardio load. The language model has two jobs only. It chooses which of a fixed set of card types to show and fills in their slots from structured data, and it writes the one or two plain sentences that explain a proposal. It never invents a number, an exercise outside the reviewed library, or a medical statement. This follows from the research on generative UI, which Google describes as slower (a minute or more in some cases) and occasionally inaccurate compared with human-built interfaces ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)). A fixed card catalogue rendered natively in Flutter is fast, testable and works offline with templated sentences when the model is unreachable.

### Card types

| Card | When it appears | Contents | Taps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal | Engine finds a change worth making (plateau, out-of-band muscle, schedule mismatch, deload due) | Headline ("Add Romanian deadlift to Thursday, 3 × 8"); the diff (before and after rows); one evidence chip ("Hamstrings 6 sets this week, band 10 to 20"); AI tag | Apply (Signal), Edit (opens steppers for sets, reps, day), Why, Not now |
| Question | The engine's choice depends on something only the user knows | One question; 3 to 5 option chips plus Skip; a line saying why it asks | One chip; Skip |
| Pain check | User flagged pain, or a follow-up is due | Body plate, then severity, type, timing and red-flag steps (below) | Taps through steps; ends in a Proposal or a Safety card |
| Applied | The coach made an auto-applied change | What changed, why, when; countdown-free Undo | Undo; Why |
| Insight | A notable fact with no action (a PR, a recovery dip) | One sentence and the number on its Scale | Dismiss |
| Safety | A red flag was reported | Stop instruction, see-a-professional advice, which exercises are paused | Understood; Resume this exercise (only after a later check-in) |
| Check-in | Scheduled follow-up (pain the next day, return after a gap, end of a deload) | One question with chips | One chip |

Each card maps to the Microsoft guidelines that the research could verify on the page: efficient dismissal (Not now), efficient correction (Edit), clear reasons (Why), conveying consequences (the diff shows exactly what changes), and notifying users about changes (Applied cards and the log) ([Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-design/)). Guideline 10, scope services when in doubt, becomes the Question card: when the engine cannot pick between two options with the data it has, it asks instead of guessing.

The question budget is a design judgement with no study behind it: one question per card, no more than three question cards a day, never ask what the data already shows, and remember answers so the same question does not return. The research found no sourced work on question fatigue or tap-based pain input.

### What the coach may change without asking

| Level | Changes | Why at this level |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic, logged, undoable | Next-set and next-session load within one increment of the program's progression rule (for example +2.5kg after the top of the rep range at the target RIR); rounding to the user's available plates and dumbbells; warm-up sets; rest-timer length within the program's range; reordering today's exercises when the user marks equipment busy | Small, reversible, inside rules the user already accepted when the program was set |
| One tap to apply | Swapping an exercise; adding or removing sets; moving exercises between days; changing rep ranges; scheduling a deload; re-entry weeks after a gap; cardio targets; any response to a plateau | Changes program structure or weekly volume; the user should see the diff first |
| Never automatic; confirmation plus safety rules | Anything following a pain report; resuming a paused exercise; any change while a red flag is open | Body-related and higher stakes; the PAIR guidance suggests prompting verification in high-stakes cases and not showing a "what" without a "why" ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)) |

A global control in Settings switches the first level off ("Ask before changing weights"), which follows HAX guideline 17 on global controls. Whoop lets users turn its coach off entirely ([Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/press-center/whoop-unveils-the-new-whoop-coach-powered-by-openai/)); Delt Arena offers the same, leaving the logger, the program and the stats working without the AI layer. Oura lets users review and delete what its advisor remembers ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-advisor)); Delt Arena's Settings has a "What the coach knows" list (goal, injuries, equipment, answers to past questions) where each item can be removed.

Auto-progression of load is also where the evidence is most comfortable. Progression by reps in reserve performed at least as well as fixed percentages in the study cited by the earlier concept ([Helms 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.00247/pdf)), and self-reported RIR is usually within about one rep of the truth ([Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve)), which is accurate enough to drive one-increment changes.

### Showing the reasoning

Each proposal carries one evidence chip in plain words ("3 sessions at 2 reps in reserve on the top set"). Tapping Why expands a panel with three parts: the data used (a small chart or table of the relevant sessions), the rule applied in one sentence ("When every set reaches the top of the range at the target effort, the load goes up one step"), and the data sufficiency ("Based on 3 sessions" or "Based on 1 session, so this is a guess"). The research guidance is to show confidence only where it changes the decision ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)); a count of sessions is easier to understand than a percentage and tells the user when to doubt the proposal.

### Undo and the activity log

Every change the coach makes, automatic or tapped, is stored as a versioned diff of the program with before, after, reason, data references, time and status (applied, undone, superseded). The Activity log on the Coach screen lists them in order. Undo on any row restores the previous version of the affected items and records the undo as its own entry. Undo stays available until a later change depends on it (for example, after a session has been logged with the new load, undoing the load change only affects future sessions, and the row says so). The research found no product example of an agent activity log with revert, so this is design judgement built on HAX guidelines 9, 16 and 18.

### Pain reporting on the body map

Pain can be flagged from the Pain key in the live screen's bottom tray, by saying it in voice mode ("left shoulder hurts", which pre-marks the region), or from the Coach screen. In every case the Coach screen opens over the current screen with a Pain check card, so the coach stays in its one screen and the session resumes underneath when the card is done. The flow is five steps, each one tap, and the user can stop after any step.

Step one is location: the front and back body plate, with large regions (shoulder, elbow, wrist, upper back, lower back, hip, knee, ankle, chest, neck) and left and right sides. A tap marks the region with an Alert registration mark and zooms to sub-areas (front, side, back of the shoulder). Step two is severity: an 11-point row of numbered chips from 0 to 10 with anchors at 0 ("none"), 5 ("hard to ignore") and 10 ("worst imaginable"). Step three is character: sharp, dull ache, pinching, burning or tingling. Step four is timing: during the lift, after the set, at rest, or at night. Step five is a red-flag checklist with a "None of these" chip: numbness or tingling spreading down a limb, pain after a fall or impact, chest pain or pressure, dizziness or shortness of breath, swelling or deformity.

The decision rules use the Silbernagel pain-monitoring model, which allows pain up to 5 out of 10 during or just after loading as long as it settles to baseline by the next morning (some clinicians use 3) ([BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12891-021-04553-6/tables/1)). That model comes from tendon rehabilitation; applying it to general gym pain is a judgement the research could not support with a source, so the app uses the stricter 3 out of 10 threshold. If severity is 3 or below, the pain is a dull ache, it occurs only during the lift and no red flag is ticked, the coach proposes one of: reduce load 10 to 20%, shorten range of motion, or swap to a variation that loads the area differently (for a shoulder pinch on incline press, a neutral-grip dumbbell press), each as a Proposal card with Apply. A check-in card appears the next morning ("Left shoulder today: better, same, worse"). If severity is 4 or more, or the pain is sharp, burning or tingling, the coach proposes skipping that exercise for today and pauses it until a check-in reports improvement. If any red flag is ticked, a Safety card replaces all proposals. For chest pain, pressure, dizziness or sudden breathlessness it tells the user to stop exercising now and seek medical help, following ACSM's advice that chest symptoms during exercise call for stopping and contacting a health provider ([ACSM](https://acsm.org/docs/default-source/files-for-resource-library/cpi-who-needs-a-stress-test-before-exercising-brochure.pdf)). For the other red flags it pauses exercises loading that region and advises seeing a physiotherapist or doctor. No progressive-load proposal touching that region appears while a red flag is open.

The coach never names a condition. Copy says "pain at the front of your left shoulder", never "impingement" or "tendinitis".

## Exercise demonstrations: line-drawn Rive animations over a public-domain seed

The demonstration is the one place where users expect motion, and the research narrows the options sharply. The only exercise library whose licence could be verified on the page is free-exercise-db, released under the Unlicense as an "Open Public Domain Exercise Dataset" with over 800 exercises, each stored as a pair of JPG images (start and end positions) ([GitHub: free-exercise-db](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db)). It has no motion, and the repository page does not state a separate licence for the images, so each image still needs a check. wger's data is Creative Commons per entry and its code is AGPL ([wger README](https://github.com/wger-project/wger/blob/master/README.md)), which makes it usable only after a per-record licence audit. The hosted GIF libraries could not be verified: ExerciseDB (now AscendAPI) shows no commercial terms in anything reachable, MuscleWiki's pricing page (seen only as a search snippet) says every paid plan includes commercial use rights ([MuscleWiki API pricing](https://api.musclewiki.com/pricing)), and API Ninjas has two terms versions that disagree ([API Ninjas](https://api-ninjas.com/terms)). Mixamo characters can be used commercially per community answers, but redistribution and bulk packaging are restricted ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/td-p/13234775)).

The recommendation follows from the licences and from the design language. Delt Arena should draw its own demonstrations as line animations in Rive, in the same engraved line style as the Body plate, so the demonstration matches the rest of the instrument. Rive's Flutter runtime is MIT-licensed and published by Rive ([pub.dev: rive](https://pub.dev/packages/rive)); Lottie's runtime is also MIT ([pub.dev: lottie](https://pub.dev/packages/lottie)). Vector animation is far smaller than GIF (one benchmark measured 46.2KB Lottie against 164.5KB GIF for the same animation, seen as a search snippet) ([Callstack](https://callstack.com/blog/lottie-vs-rive-optimizing-mobile-app-animation)). Rive is preferred over Lottie because a Rive state machine can hold the still start and end poses, the loop, and a highlighted working muscle in one file, which covers reduced motion without a second asset. The working muscle is drawn in the Band shade during the concentric phase, so the animation also teaches what the exercise trains.

At launch, the 120 or so most-used exercises (the ones in generated programs) get Rive animations. Every other exercise shows the free-exercise-db start and end images side by side, redrawn or tinted into the line style where the licence check allows, with three written cues. Filmed video is not needed for launch; if added later for complex lifts, MP4 through `video_player` (BSD-3, ExoPlayer on Android) is the practical route ([pub.dev: video_player](https://pub.dev/packages/video_player)). MuscleWiki or another hosted library is worth licensing only if the in-house library cannot cover the long tail in time, and only with written commercial terms.

Delivery is offline-first. The 120 launch animations ship inside the app bundle (each Rive file is small; the total size must be measured, since no figure was found). Long-tail media downloads on first view into the app's documents directory, not the cache: `flutter_cache_manager` stores files in the temporary directory by default, where the OS may delete them at any time ([pub.dev: flutter_cache_manager](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_cache_manager)). A "Download all demonstrations" switch in Settings serves users with poor gym signal. When the system's remove-animations setting is on, or the user turns off motion in Settings, the Exercise sheet shows the start and end poses side by side with an arrow between them, and the animation plays only on an explicit tap. Each demonstration has a text description for screen readers ("Barbell bench press: lower the bar to mid-chest, press up until arms are straight").

## A stats system that answers five questions in one place each

The Read loop exists to answer five questions: did this week work, is each lift improving, are my muscles trained in balance, is my cardio fitness improving, and is my body changing at the rate I want. Each has its own screen, and the Week screen answers all five in summary. Every chart follows the same rules: a one-sentence takeaway above it written by the engine (with the AI layer allowed only to rephrase), the number on its Scale with its band where a band exists, tap-to-scrub with 48dp targets instead of hover, a "view as table" switch, and a semantic label that states the takeaway, as chart-accessibility guidance recommends ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2024/02/accessibility-standards-empower-better-chart-visual-design)). Radar charts are not used; sorted horizontal bars with bands carry the same information more precisely. Tapping any number opens a short panel with its formula and the data behind it, because opaque scores are a known complaint about competitors ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/08/19/garmin-body-battery-slammed-indirectly-by-altini-made-up-scores/)).

| Category | Measure and formula | Chart form | What the coach draws from it |

| Body: protein | Info only: 1.6 g per kg per day, the point beyond which a meta-regression found no further muscle gain ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)); the ISSN range is 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg ([ISSN](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/)) | Single number with band on the Body screen | Insight card, never a meal plan |

Health Connect provides the recovery inputs as separate types (SleepSession, RestingHeartRate, HeartRateVariabilityRmssd and others), each with its own read permission, and reading data older than 30 days needs the separate history permission ([HC data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/data-types)). The Recovery screen therefore asks for permissions only when opened, and its empty state names the missing source ("Connect a watch or ring that writes sleep to Health Connect").

The Week screen arranges the summary for a Sunday reading. At the top is one sentence ("4 of 4 sessions, 71 hard sets, hamstrings under range, bench up 2.5 kg"). Below it, in order: sessions against plan on the week strip; hard sets per muscle as sorted bars with bands; PR stamps; cardio minutes against the WHO band; bodyweight trend rate; recovery deltas. Each row opens its detail screen. On tablets and foldables, Week is the list and the detail opens beside it.

Hevy's free tier limits graph history to three months ([Hevy help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35119778922263)); Delt Arena keeps all history and allows CSV export, since the existence of LiftShift suggests users want their data out of their logger.

## Machine cardio: connect, photograph, or type four fields

No source describes how any app handles gym machine cardio, so this loop is designed from the protocols and from the motive: the user wants the machine's numbers saved without retyping them. The Cardio start screen tries the cheapest path first and falls back in a fixed order: a Bluetooth connection to the machine; a photo of the console at the end; a short manual form. Heart rate comes from a strap or armband over Bluetooth, or from a watch through Health Connect afterwards.

Bluetooth FTMS (Fitness Machine Service, current revision 1.0.1 from October 2024) defines optional data characteristics per machine type: treadmill, cross trainer, step climber, stair climber, rower and indoor bike ([Bluetooth SIG](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/fitness-machine-service-1-0/), [FTMS ICS](https://files.bluetooth.com/download/ftms-ics-p4-pdf)). Because each type's characteristic is optional, the app parses per characteristic and shows whatever the machine sends. Vendor support is confirmed for Matrix consumer treadmills, bikes and ellipticals ([Matrix](https://matrixhomefitness.com/pages/never-a-dull-workout)) and Life Fitness's Track Connect 2.0 console ([Life Fitness](https://www.lifefitness.com/en-eu/catalog/cardio/consoles/track-connect-2-0-console)), and Technogym's Mywellness reads any FTMS machine ([Mywellness](https://www.mywellness.com/de-DE/integrations)). Support on StairMaster, Precor, Keiser, Peloton and Echelon machines is not established. The FTMS byte layouts could not be fetched and must be read from the official specification before parsers are written.

Concept2's PM5 monitor (rowers, ski ergs and BikeErg) speaks its own Bluetooth protocol, documented in a PM5 interface definition and implemented by the open-source c2bluetooth library ([c2bluetooth](https://github.com/OpenRowingCommunity/c2bluetooth), [Concept2 software development](https://www.concept2.com/support/software-development)). The Logbook API offers authoritative results and stroke data after the workout; live use requires approval from Concept2 ([Concept2 Logbook API](https://log.concept2.com/developers/documentation)). The plan is live PM5 data on the Cardio live screen, then optional Logbook reconciliation for users who connect an account.

The photo path uses ML Kit text recognition, which runs on device and offline on Android API 21 and up, with accuracy that depends on image quality ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/android)). Gym consoles often use seven-segment or dot-matrix digits, on which a general text model is untested, so every parsed field shows its value with a "check" mark when confidence is low, and the user confirms before saving. The camera step can always be skipped.

| Machine | Connection path | Minimum manual fields | Calorie estimate (shown beside the machine's own figure) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treadmill | FTMS Treadmill Data | Duration, distance, incline | ACSM walking (VO2 = 0.1 × speed + 1.8 × speed × grade + 3.5 ml/kg/min, speed in m/min) under about 4 mph; running (0.2 × speed + 0.9 × speed × grade + 3.5) above 5 mph ([UNM equations](https://www.unm.edu/%7errobergs/478Steady%20State%20VO2%20Prediction%20Equations.doc)); kcal ≈ VO2 × kg × min ÷ 1000 × 5 |
| Stair climber / step mill | FTMS Stair or Step Climber Data where supported; otherwise photo or manual | Duration, floors (or steps), level | Compendium 2024 stair-treadmill MET 9.3 × kg × hours ([Compendium 2024](https://pacompendium.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2024-adult-compendium_1_2024.pdf)) |
| Rower | PM5 protocol; FTMS Rower Data for other brands | Duration, distance, average split | From watts by Concept2's formula, or Compendium rowing METs by wattage band (11.0 for 150 to 199 W, 14.0 for 200 W and over) |
| Ski erg | PM5 protocol | Duration, distance, average split | PM5 value; no MET source was checked |
| Stationary or spin bike | FTMS Indoor Bike Data | Duration, average watts or resistance level | From watts when present; otherwise Compendium cycling METs (6.8 general, 10.3 for 151 to 199 W) |
| Air bike | Photo or manual (protocol support unknown) | Duration, calories from console, average RPM or watts | Console value labelled "machine estimate"; no validated formula found |
| Elliptical | FTMS Cross Trainer Data | Duration, distance, resistance | Console value plus a warning, since one study found ellipticals overestimate by about 100 kcal per 30 minutes ([Exercise Medicine 2018](https://doaj.org/article/37d08913b6004a4f901ea440279679ef)); no elliptical MET was retrieved |
| Incline trainer | FTMS Treadmill Data | Duration, speed, incline | ACSM walking equation |

Optional on every machine: average heart rate, effort 1 to 10, note. Calories are the weakest number in the app. One thesis on a single treadmill model found console calories 67 to 117% too high against a metabolic analyser ([University of Wisconsin](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/bf175739-501d-4327-91e7-c7004b4474c3)), the ACSM equations were derived from a small, narrow sample and disagree with each other at 8 km/h ([Frontiers 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1522214/pdf)), and the Compendium says it was not designed to measure precise energy cost. Delt Arena therefore shows both the machine's figure and its own estimate, labels both as estimates, lets the user pick which one goes into stats and Health Connect, and uses minutes, work and heart rate (not calories) for every coach decision. No heart-rate-based calorie equation was sourced, so none is used.

Heart-rate straps and armbands use the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate service, which the research could not verify against the specification; pairing must be tested on common straps. Health Connect has heart rate as a standard type, so watch heart rate can be read after the session and attached to it ([HC data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/data-types)). Each cardio session is written to Health Connect as an `ExerciseSessionRecord` of the matching type with its heart rate, distance, power and calories, using `clientRecordId` to avoid duplicates and avoiding overlap with sessions the user's watch also records ([HC workouts](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/experiences/workouts)).

## Onboarding builds a program from templates and checks every edit against ranges

### Setup in six questions

Setup asks only what changes the program, one question per screen, with large tap targets. Fitbod asks goal and experience and keeps equipment profiles ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner)); Gravl builds a first gym profile from onboarding answers ([Gravl help](https://intercom.help/gravl/en/articles/10185534-gravl-essentials-set-up-your-fitness-plan)). Neither publishes a step count or time, so the two-minute target is judgement.

| Step | Question | Options | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Disclosure | One screen: what Delt Arena stores, that Coach suggestions are AI-generated and rule-checked, general-wellness note | Nothing; required for Article 50 and Play policy |
| 1 | Goal | Fat loss, build muscle (bulk), recomposition, maintain muscle, hypertrophy focus, strength, general fitness | Rep ranges, weekly set band, cardio share, body rate band |
| 2 | Body | Sex (or prefer not to say), age, height, weight (import from Health Connect offered), training age (new, under 1 year, 1 to 3 years, 3+ years) | Starting volume, starting loads, calorie estimates |
| 3 | Schedule | Days per week (2 to 6), session length (30, 45, 60, 75+ minutes), preferred cardio machines and minutes | Split template, exercises per session |

| 6 | Program reveal | The week grid, per-muscle Scales filling to their bands, first session preview | Start plan |

Experienced lifters can skip to "Import or build my own" at step 1.

### How the program is generated

The engine fills a known template structure; the language model never invents a split. Templates are chosen so each muscle is trained at least twice a week, the frequency with the best support ([PubMed 27102172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)): full body twice for 2 days, full body three times for 3, upper and lower twice for 4, upper-lower plus a push-pull-legs hybrid for 5, and push-pull-legs twice for 6. ACSM's position stand suggests 2 to 3 sessions per week for novices, 3 to 4 for intermediates and 4 to 5 for advanced lifters ([PubMed 19204579](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/)), so the engine nudges a new lifter who picks 6 days towards 3 or 4 with a one-line reason and lets them keep 6.

Weekly sets start at the lower half of the band and rise over a mesocycle. More weekly sets give more hypertrophy with diminishing returns, and the data are thin above about 10 sets per muscle per week ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://researchgate.net/profile/Brad-Schoenfeld/publication/305455324_Dose-response_relationship_between_weekly_resistance_training_volume_and_increases_in_muscle_mass_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis/links/59dc0269458515e9ab4527d6/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-resistance-training-volume-and-increases-in-muscle-mass-A-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis.pdf)); Schoenfeld suggests 10 to 20 sets per muscle per week in interviews ([FitnessVolt](https://fitnessvolt.com/dr-schoenfeld-examines-ideal-set-range-for-maximizing-hypertrophy/)). Rep ranges follow the goal: hypertrophy is similar across a wide load range when sets are hard, while strength favours heavier loads ([PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/), [PubMed 33433148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/)). Most sets stop 1 to 3 reps short of failure, since training to failure gave only a trivial hypertrophy advantage in a 2023 meta-analysis ([PubMed 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)).

| Goal | Rep ranges (compound / isolation) | Weekly fractional sets per muscle | Cardio | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy, bulk | 6 to 12 / 10 to 20 | Start 10, ceiling 20 | 2 sessions, low impact preferred | Running was most detrimental in an older concurrent-training meta-analysis ([bib record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1027372546)), though a 2021 meta-analysis found no interference with hypertrophy or maximal strength ([Sports Medicine 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7)) |
| Strength | 3 to 6 on main lifts / 8 to 12 accessories | 8 to 14 | 2 sessions, away from heavy leg days | Direct sets counted for main lifts |
| Fat loss | 6 to 12 / 10 to 15 | 10 to 14 (keep the stimulus) | Up to WHO band | Body rate band 0.5 to 1% per week |
| Recomposition | 6 to 12 / 10 to 20 | 10 to 16 | 2 to 3 sessions | Best suited to new and returning lifters (weakly sourced) |
| Maintain muscle | 6 to 12 / 10 to 15 | 4 to 8, 1 to 2 sessions per muscle | User's choice | Reduced doses maintained muscle in young adults but not in older adults in one trial ([PubMed 21131862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/)), so users over 60 keep more volume |
| General fitness | 8 to 15 | 6 to 10 | WHO band of 150 to 300 moderate minutes ([BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/24/1451)) | Whole-body template |

Deloads are scheduled every 5 to 7 weeks or proposed reactively; a Delphi consensus defines a deload as a period of reduced training to manage fatigue, and coaches most often use one week ([PMC10511399](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10511399/)). The interval and the reduction (about half the sets at the same load) are judgement.

### Warnings for user-built programs

The Program editor shows each muscle's weekly fractional sets on a Scale at the top of the screen, updating as exercises are added, so the user sees the effect before saving. Rules fire as text warnings under the Scales with the Over or Under marker. The user is never blocked: each warning has "Keep as is", which records the choice so the warning does not return for that program. None of the thresholds was found in a tested source; they are derived from the ranges above and labelled in the app as guidelines.

| Rule | Threshold | Warning text (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly sets too high | Over 20 fractional sets for a muscle (over 25 is shown as strongly high) | "Chest: 34 sets a week. Gains level off well below this, and recovery may limit your other lifts." |
| Weekly sets too low | Under 6 for a muscle the goal targets | "Hamstrings: 3 sets a week. Most programs for growth use at least 10." |
| Session sets too high | Over 10 fractional sets for one muscle in one session, a practitioner estimate of productive per-session volume ([Henselmans](https://mennohenselmans.com/maximum-productive-training-volume-per-session/)) | "Monday has 16 sets for chest. Splitting them across two days usually works better." |
| Frequency | A goal muscle trained on only one day | "Back is trained once a week. Twice a week did better in studies with the same total sets." |
| Rest days | No rest day in 7, or the same muscle hard on consecutive days | "Legs are trained hard on Monday and Tuesday." |
| Imbalance | Quads over band while hamstrings under, or push more than twice pull | "Quads 22 sets, hamstrings 6." (no ratio target claimed) |
| Effort | Every set to failure | "All sets go to failure. Leaving 1 to 3 reps gives nearly the same growth with less fatigue." |
| Cardio spike | Planned weekly cardio well above the last 4 weeks' average | "Cardio doubles this week. Consider building up over a few weeks." |

## Eight moments, walked through step by step

### First open

Maya installs Delt Arena on a Pixel and opens it. The first screen is Paper with a single sentence on what the app stores and a line saying Coach suggestions are generated by AI and checked against training rules; she taps Continue. Goal: she taps "Build muscle". Body: she enters height and weight with the Ruler, taps "Import weight from Health Connect" which raises the system permission dialog for weight only, and picks "1 to 3 years" of training. Schedule: 4 days, 60 minutes, and the stair climber for 15 minutes twice a week. Equipment: "Commercial gym", smallest plate 1.25kg. Injuries: she taps the left shoulder on the body plate and chooses "Go easy". The reveal draws a four-day upper-lower week; the Scales for each muscle fill to 10 to 12 sets, and the shoulder region shows a note that overhead pressing starts with dumbbells. A Margin note under the week says "AI · Starting volume is at the low end; it rises over 5 weeks." She taps Start plan, lands on Today with "Upper A · 58 min" and a Start button, and is offered the widget once. No paywall or account is required to log; pricing is an open question.

### A normal push day with supersets

On Thursday Maya taps Start on Upper A. The live screen shows "Warm-up 1 of 3 · 40 kg" with the plates per side ("20 kg bar + 10"); she taps anywhere three times through the warm-ups. The first working set shows "65 kg · 8 · 2 in reserve"; she does 8 and taps. A Stamp pulse, the numbers slide into the strip, rest starts at 2:30 and the Live Update chip shows 2:29 on her lock screen. She pockets the phone; at 0:00 it double-pulses. The centre now predicts set 2: "65 · 7". She does 8 again, presses and holds, the keypad rises with 7 filled, she taps 8 and Log. On set 3 the engine is unsure after her extra rep, so the screen splits: "8 likely" on the right, "7 possible" on the left. She gets 7 and taps the left half. After the last set, the rest screen asks "How many more could you have done?" and she taps 1. A Margin note appears in the stamp strip: "AI · Next week 67.5 kg" with Undo, an automatic change inside the program's rule.

Next is a superset: A1 incline dumbbell press, A2 cable fly. The top bar reads "A1 of 2". She taps after A1, and the centre switches straight to A2 with no rest; she taps after A2 and rest starts with A1's next set preloaded underneath. At the end the centre reads "Tap to finish session"; the Summary shows 16 hard sets, chest and shoulders hatched on the body plate, one PR stamp, and "Saved to Health Connect".

### A StairMaster session

On Saturday Maya taps the cardio shortcut on Today and picks Stair climber. The app scans for 10 seconds, finds no FTMS machine (StairMaster support is unknown), and offers "Track time now, add numbers at the end". She taps Start; her chest strap pairs and the Cardio live screen shows elapsed time and heart rate on a zone Scale. After 20 minutes she taps Finish. The Console capture screen opens the camera with a framing guide; she photographs the console. ML Kit reads "20:03", "62 FLOORS" and "LEVEL 8" and a calorie figure of 241. The floors field shows a "check" mark because one digit was low confidence; she taps it, sees 62 is right, and taps Save values. The Summary shows floors per minute, average heart rate, and two calorie figures: "Machine 241 · Estimate 198 (MET 9.3 × 68 kg × 0.33 h)". She picks Estimate for her stats. The session goes to Health Connect as a stair-climbing machine session with her heart rate attached.

### Reporting left-shoulder pain mid-set

The next Tuesday, during set 2 of incline dumbbell press, Maya feels a pinch at the front of her left shoulder. She racks the dumbbells and taps Pain in the bottom tray. The Coach screen opens over the session with a Pain check card and the body plate. She taps the left shoulder, then "Front". Severity: she taps 3. Character: "Pinching". Timing: "During the lift". Red flags: "None of these". The card turns into a Proposal: "Swap to neutral-grip dumbbell press for today, same weight, and stop each rep a hand's width above the chest", with Why showing "Pain 3/10, during the lift, no red flags; guideline keeps pain at or below 3." She taps Apply. The session resumes underneath with the swapped exercise, and an Applied card sits in the log. The next morning a Check-in card asks "Left shoulder today?" and she taps "Better". Had she tapped "Numbness or tingling", a Safety card would have paused all pressing for that shoulder and advised seeing a physiotherapist or doctor, with no load proposals until a later check-in.

In voice mode the same moment is shorter: she presses the mic and says "left shoulder hurts"; the agent opens the Pain check with the left shoulder already marked, and she taps through the remaining four steps.

### A plateau week

Over five weeks Maya's barbell bench e1RM has stayed at 82 to 83kg across seven sessions, with a slope of zero. On Sunday the Coach screen shows a Proposal: "Bench press has not improved in 5 weeks." Why shows the Lift chart with the flat region shaded, "7 sessions, best e1RM 83 kg twice", and a check of recovery inputs ("Sleep and resting heart rate within your normal range"), so the stall is not blamed on poor sleep. The card offers three chips: "Change rep range to 8 to 10 for 3 weeks", "Add one set", "Swap to close-grip bench for 4 weeks". She taps the first. The diff shows bench moving from 4 × 6 to 8 at 65kg to 3 × 8 to 10 at 60kg. She taps Apply, and the change is logged with Undo.

### Returning after two weeks off

Maya comes back after 15 days away. Today shows her next session with a Margin note ("AI · 15 days since your last session"), and the Coach badge shows 1. The Question card asks "What kept you away?" with chips: Holiday, Ill, Injury, Busy. She taps Ill. The Proposal follows: "Re-entry week: same exercises, about 90% of your last loads, one set fewer per exercise. Back to plan next week if it goes well." The percentages are judgement; no source on detraining after two weeks was retrieved, though reduced doses maintained strength and size in young adults in one trial ([PubMed 21131862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/)). She taps Apply. Her consistency streak is counted in weeks that met the plan, and the plan was paused while she was ill, so the streak shows "paused", not broken. Had she tapped Injury, the Pain check would have started.

### Building a custom program that is over-volume

Sam, an experienced lifter, opens Plan, taps New program and chooses blank. He builds a five-day bro split. On chest day he adds flat bench 5 × 8, incline bench 5 × 8, dumbbell press 4 × 10, cable fly 4 × 12, pec deck 4 × 15 and dips 4 × 10. As he adds them, the chest Scale at the top climbs past the band and turns to the Over marker at 26 sets; triceps also creep up from the pressing at 0.5 per set. Three warnings appear: "Chest: 26 fractional sets this week", "Monday has 26 sets for chest in one session", and "Chest is trained once a week. Twice a week did better with the same total sets." Each has "Keep as is" and a suggestion chip ("Move incline and cable fly to Thursday"). Sam keeps the volume but taps the suggestion to split it, which removes the second and third warnings. He taps Save program. The coach will not bring the volume up again unless his chest lifts stall or his recovery inputs fall.

## Build order and the technical decisions that carry risk

### Build order

| Phase | Scope | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Foundations | Design tokens for Tolerance (colour roles, type, springs, haptics) in the app's own design-system package; local database; exercise library with per-muscle weights (1.0 and 0.5) reviewed by a coach; rule engine as pure Dart with tests | Everything else depends on the library, the engine and the tokens |
| 1. Logging core | Live set screen in tap mode, Thumb keypad, prediction, split mode; rest timer in a foreground service with Live Update; Session summary; History with editing; offline-first storage | Reliable logging is what users judge first, and the rest-timer bugs in competitors show how visible failure is |
| 2. Plan | Setup, template generator, Plan and Program editor with warnings | Gives the engine a program to progress |
| 3. Read | Week, Lift, Muscles, Body; CSV export | Needs only logged data |
| 4. Coach | Coach screen with engine-generated cards and templated text; then the LLM explanation layer; Pain check and Safety cards; activity log and Undo; disclosure and settings | The engine must be right before any AI writes about it |
| 5. Cardio and Health Connect | Manual cardio, console photo with ML Kit, BLE heart rate, Health Connect write and read; Cardio stats; Recovery | Manual and photo paths cover every machine before any protocol work |
| 6. Protocols and surfaces | FTMS parsers per machine type, Concept2 PM5 and Logbook, widgets, Quick Settings tile, Wear OS app | Highest uncertainty; specs still to be read |
| 7. Voice mode (beta) | On-device grammar, intent classifier, step-by-step agent execution, undo list; gym-noise testing | Optional mode with unmeasured accuracy |
| 8. iOS parity | HealthKit through the same plugin, Live Activities for rest, Apple Watch later | The Flutter code is shared; platform surfaces differ |

|---|---|---|
| Material 3 Expressive | Own token layer and components; evaluate `material_3_expressive` or the official `material_ui` package when stable | Flutter SDK does not ship Expressive; community package requires replacing `package:flutter/material.dart` with `material_ui` ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/material_3_expressive), [flutter#168813](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/168813)) |

| Bluetooth | `flutter_blue_plus` 2.3.15 behind a repository interface | Free only for personal, nonprofit and educational use; a for-profit app needs a paid commercial licence; Android throttles scans to 5 starts per 30 seconds; services must be rediscovered after reconnect; background BLE may need a fork ([pub.dev: flutter_blue_plus](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_blue_plus)). | Rest timer and Live Updates | Native Kotlin foreground service owning the timer; Live Update through a platform channel; Flutter UI observes it | Needs `POST_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS`, no RemoteViews, not colourised; respect demotion and do not repost a dismissed update ([Live Updates](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)). No Flutter-native Live Update package was found. |
| Widgets and tile | Native Kotlin (Glance) via platform channel or a widget bridge package | Not researched; treat as native work |
| OCR | ML Kit text recognition, unbundled model | On-device and offline; v2 labelled beta on one Google page ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/android)); seven-segment accuracy untested |
| Exercise media | `rive` (MIT) for animations; `video_player` (BSD-3) if filmed clips are added; documents-directory storage for downloads | Cache manager uses temporary storage by default ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_cache_manager)); wger data is per-entry CC and code AGPL; free-exercise-db image licence to confirm per file |
| Speech | Android on-device SpeechRecognizer where available; bundled open-source model as an option | Not researched; verify offline model availability, OEM differences and gym-noise accuracy |
| AI layer | Server-side LLM call that receives structured engine output and returns card choice and text in a fixed JSON schema; engine and templated text run on device | Generative UI is slow and occasionally wrong ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)); keep numbers out of the model's hands |
| Concept2 | c2bluetooth as a reference for PM5; Logbook API for results | Live Logbook use needs Concept2 approval ([Concept2](https://log.concept2.com/developers/documentation)) |
| Accessibility | Semantics, MergeSemantics, CustomSemanticsAction on every custom component; WCAG 2.2 AA target | EAA applies since 28 June 2025; whether it covers a fitness app needs legal confirmation ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eu-accessibility-requirements-and-eaa-compliance/)) |

State management, the local database, charting and the widget bridge were not researched; common choices (Riverpod, Drift on SQLite, a custom painter for charts so they follow Tolerance exactly) are judgement.

## Conclusion

The research points to a product whose hardest work sits in the rule engine and the live set screen. A coach is only as trusted as the numbers under it, and every number here comes from a rule that can be shown on tap, with the language model limited to choosing cards and writing sentences. The biggest daily saving comes from predicting the next set well enough that one tap is usually right, and the plan measures that directly: the share of sets logged with a single tap is the metric to watch in beta, ahead of any engagement figure for the Coach.

Tolerance gives the app one rule that every screen can follow: show each number against its range. That rule suits the subject, since the training evidence is itself a set of ranges with uncertain edges, and it lets the app be honest about uncertainty in the same visual terms it uses for progress. The unresolved work is concentrated in a few blocked sources (machine protocols, the Expressive spec, media licences, speech accuracy), and each can be closed with a document or a device test before the phase that needs it.
