# Google Play reviews: Alpha Progression, JEFIT, Gravl (gap B)

Collected 26 Nov 2026 through the user's own Chrome (Tether, bypass mode), United States English storefront. Read-only. No login, install, or submission.

## Method

Each app page was opened, then "See all reviews" was opened and the dialog was scrolled to load more reviews. The dialog's default sort, "Most relevant", was used, so the sample leans toward reviews other users found helpful. Ratings per review came from the star labels in the page. Theme counts come from a keyword match over the full review text of the loaded sample. The matches are broad (for example "plan", "rest", "free"), so treat the counts as rough. They show frequency in the sample, not measured prevalence.

Samples loaded: Alpha Progression 340 reviews, JEFIT 480 reviews, Gravl 80 reviews. These are small fractions of the totals, and none of the samples is a random draw.

## Alpha Progression

Package: com.alphaprogression.alphaprogression. Store page shows 4.8 stars and 21.5K reviews (dialog header shows 21.3K).

Sample of 340, by star: 5 stars 300, 4 stars 27, 3 stars 4, 2 stars 3, 1 star 6. Sample dates run from April 2021 to November 2026, and 139 of the 340 are dated 2026.

Praise, by rough count across the 340 sampled reviews:
- Plan building and progression (about 174 mention plans, auto recommendations, progression or generator terms). Reviewers praise weight and rep suggestions. One November 2026 five-star review says the app is "very accurate in picking weights and rep ranges".
- Exercise variety and media (about 133 mention videos, library, or custom exercises). Reviewers like the large library and adding custom exercises.
- Stats (about 126 mention stats, charts, volume or trends). Reviewers like the muscle-by-muscle set counts.
- Logging speed (about 24 mention quick, fast or efficient logging). One reviewer says they "log my workouts quickly and efficiently". Another says they can track weight, reps and sets "without having to faff about too long on logging".
- Free tier usefulness is praised in several reviews (customising a plan and seeing last week's weight and reps on the free version).

Complaints:
- Paywall and price (about 110 mention paid, subscription, premium or free). Most are neutral. Some one-star reviews complain about subscription prompts.
- Rest timer and audio (about 31 mention rest or timer; about 13 mention audio, notification, reminder or alert). The clearest complaint is a September 2026 one-star review saying audio notifications stopped after an update. The developer replied on 27 September 2026 that a fix was on its way. A May 2026 three-star review says there is "no audible alert" when rest ends after an update. A 2025 two-star review says rest notifications follow the ringer volume rather than the media volume.
- Editing during a workout (about 18 mention removing, deleting or editing sets). A November 2026 five-star reviewer found it hard to remove an extra set they had added during a workout.
- UI (about 61 mention layout, interface or confusing). One reviewer wrote that "the UI is a little bit confusing sometimes".
- Bugs (about 6 mention bugs, crashes or freezes). A November 2026 three-star review says the app freezes and has to be restarted during workouts.
- Wear OS and widgets (about 11 mention watch or widget). Reviewers ask for a Garmin integration.
- Import and export: one review asks for Google Sheets import. The 2026 changelog adds CSV export.

Recent reviews (last 20 by date, all from 2026): 19 of 20 are five stars, and one is three stars (freezing). Most recent reviews are about plans, progression and exercise choice, not about logging speed or the timer.

Sources: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alphaprogression.alphaprogression&hl=en&gl=US

## JEFIT

Package: je.fit. Store page shows 4.4 stars and 90.2K reviews (dialog header shows 88.1K).

Sample of 480, by star: 5 stars 182, 4 stars 83, 3 stars 69, 2 stars 48, 1 star 98. Dates run from 2019 to November 2026, and 179 of the 480 are dated 2026. The 1-star share is much higher than in Alpha's sample, and the sample includes many 2019 to 2021 reviews from older app versions.

Praise, by rough count across the 480 sampled reviews:
- Exercise database and free tools (about 122 mention exercises, library, video or database). Reviewers praise custom exercises and the free tier.
- Stats and history (about 84 mention stats, progress, volume or trends).
- Plan and AI features (about 136 mention plan, generator, progression, recommendations or AI). Some reviewers like the AI rep counter. Others say "not sure if I trust the automatic progression feature" but have kept using the app for years.
- Logging speed (about 24 mention quick, fast or efficient logging).

Complaints:
- Paywall (about 139 mention paid, subscription, price, premium or free). This is the largest complaint group. A November 2026 two-star review says a recent update put Samsung watch access behind a paywall. A 2023 reviewer says features are locked behind the paid tier.
- Bugs, sync and end-of-workout flow (about 47 mention bugs, crashes, sync or freezes). The most recent one-star reviews, both dated 24 November 2026, say that after the last update it is "impossible to end a workout", because the app keeps asking for an extra set. Other November 2026 reviews report that the app cannot end and save a session, and that the app crashes on open.
- Rest timer (about 46 mention rest or timer). A 2026 reviewer wants the timer to keep running when they leave the screen. A November 2026 review says the rest timer between watch and phone is glitchy.
- Notifications and audio (about 18 mention notifications, sound, reminder or alert).
- Watch and widgets (about 26 mention a watch, Wear OS or widget; this is a broad match, and some mentions are about Samsung watch). The Play page's changelog says Wear OS users now see live heart rate and calories on the phone during a workout.
- Input friction: a 2024 four-star review asks for weight and rep sliders back, because typing numbers into each field during a workout is tedious. The paraphrase is that sliders were faster than typing.
- UI (about 62 mention layout, interface or clunky). Several long-time users say updates made editing plans and exercises harder.
- Ads: some free-tier reviewers mention ads.

Sources: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=je.fit&hl=en&gl=US

## Gravl

Package: com.liteup.getgains (the separate app "Gravl: Macros", com.gravl.macros, is a different product and was not used). Store page shows 4.9 stars and 18.3K reviews (dialog header shows 18.2K).

Sample of 80, by star: 5 stars 52, 4 stars 7, 3 stars 7, 2 stars 3, 1 star 11. The dialog stopped loading at 80 reviews. The sample is small and skews recent (mostly July to November 2026).

Praise, by rough count across the 80 sampled reviews:
- AI plan and guided workouts (about 24 mention AI, plan, coach or progression). Beginners praise the walk-through and the "takes the guesswork out". One reviewer returning from surgery says strength went up to 50 percent in a few months.
- Exercise videos (about 18 mention videos, exercises or demos). Several reviewers say the demo videos help with form.
- Garmin integration: one five-star review says the app can start on the phone and be continued on a Garmin watch.

Complaints:
- Paywall and price (about 13 mention paid, premium, price, trial or refund). The sharpest complaints are a November 2026 one-star review about being charged after a trial with no refund, and an August 2026 two-star review that says the paid gate appeared only after setup. The highest-helpfulness review in the sample is that August two-star complaint, with 22 helpful votes.
- AI variety and equipment assumptions (several reviews). An August 2026 one-star review says the AI does not vary muscle groups across back-to-back days. A two-star review says the app programs weights that cannot be loaded because it ignores plate counts.
- Timer and bugs (about 6 mention bugs or timer). A one-star review says the rest timer disappears after a set is completed. Another says the timer sound plays only intermittently.
- Data loss (one review): measurements were said to be reset by an update.
- Logging (about 2 mention reps or sets entry). A four-star review says the app caps reps in a way that makes light-weight high-rep sets awkward to log.
- Customisation: splits are limited, and created exercises cannot be edited.

Sources: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.liteup.getgains&hl=en&gl=US

## What this means for Delt Arena

Live set screen. Logging speed is rarely praised outright in these samples (about 5 to 7 percent of reviews). The praise that does appear is that logging is quick and does not take long. The recurring friction is the end-of-workout and add or remove set flow. JEFIT's November 2026 end-workout bug and Alpha's "extra set" complaint both point to the same risk: a user who taps the wrong thing mid-set needs a fast, obvious undo. The set screen should make removing a set as easy as adding one.

Rest timer. The rest timer is a frequent complaint across all three apps, and most of those complaints are about sound. Alpha's audio alerts stopped after an update, JEFIT reviewers want the timer to keep running after leaving the screen, and Gravl's timer sound plays only intermittently. One Alpha reviewer found that the alert followed the ringer volume, so a user who had silenced their phone missed the alert. For Delt Arena, a rest timer should show a visible countdown, keep running in the background, and not depend on one sound channel. Vibration and a visual end state would cover the users who miss the audio.

One-hand tap logging. The reviews do not give direct evidence about one-hand use. What they show is that typing weights and reps into each field is tedious (JEFIT sliders request, Gravl's rep cap). A one-hand design should use large steppers or sliders for weight and reps, with the last value prefilled, so a set can be logged with a few taps.

AI coach trust. Trust is built by recommendations that match what the user can actually do, and lost when they do not. Alpha and Gravl reviewers praise accurate weight and rep picks. Gravl's complaints are about weights the user cannot load, too little exercise variety across days, and a paywall that appeared late. JEFIT users say they are not sure they trust automatic progression but keep using it. Delt Arena should show the reason behind a suggested weight, let the user set their available plates, and disclose any paid gate before setup.

Paywall timing. The paywall complaints are the most common negative theme in JEFIT and Gravl, and both apps' worst reviews are about being charged or gated after a user has invested setup time. Disclosing the paid tier before setup is an easy way to avoid that.

## Still not reached

- The "Newest" sort was not used. The samples are mostly "Most relevant", so recent-review themes are based on the 20 most recent dates within each sample, not on a full recent pull.
- Gravl's dialog stopped at 80 reviews of 18.2K. The sample is too small to support strong percentages.
- Full sampled text was parsed for all three apps, but keyword counts are approximate. No manual coding of every review was done.
- Wear OS was not checked as a separate filter. The watch counts are broad and mix Wear OS, Garmin and Samsung watch mentions.
- Rest-timer behaviour was not tested in the apps. Everything here is what reviewers reported.
- Developer replies were read only in passing for Alpha and JEFIT.
- Store ratings differ slightly between the page card and the dialog header (Alpha 21.5K vs 21.3K, JEFIT 90.2K vs 88.1K, Gravl 18.3K vs 18.2K). The card figure is quoted above and the dialog figure is shown in brackets.
- No screenshots were taken of the dialogs.
