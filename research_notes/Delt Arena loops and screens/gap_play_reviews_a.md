# Google Play reviews: Hevy, Strong, Fitbod (gap research A)

Collected 2026-11-26 from the US Play Store listings, through the user's browser (read only). Nothing was installed, purchased, posted or submitted, and no sign-in was used.

Method: each listing page was read for rating, review count and "Updated on" date. Then "See all reviews" was opened. The dialog shows the default "Most relevant" sort, not a helpfulness sort, so the "helpful" counts shown next to reviews are reported only as given. Counts below are out of the reviews in the dialog (Hevy 20, Strong about 20, Fitbod about 19). They are a sample, not the full population, and counts are approximate hand tallies. Star-filtered samples (1 star, 2 star) were not pulled.

## Hevy (com.hevy)

Listing: 4.9 stars, 295K reviews (dialog header shows 292K), 5M+ downloads, updated Nov 20, 2026, rated Teen, in-app purchases. Developer reply count in the sample: 2 (both to feature requests).

Praise: about 8 of 20 reviews praise the free tier, the low subscription price or the lack of ads. Reviewers name the $2.99 monthly plan and a lifetime purchase. About 7 of 20 praise custom exercises and custom routines. About 3 of 20 praise the per-exercise progress graphs and end-of-workout stats. About 2 of 20 praise the demo videos or form guides. One reviewer names the Wear OS app as a reason to stay. One reviewer praises routine and exercise notes.

Complaints and requests: rest timers come up in 3 of 20. One asks for per-set rest durations (Ethen Cobb). One asks for a rest timer that starts after the whole exercise, not after each set (Tall Whites, developer replied that it is noted). One asks for a rest timer visible at a glance, in the style of a status-bar timer (Mathias Olsen, the reviewer wrote "now-bar"; the meaning is an inference). Superset and set structure: 1 of 20 says supersets are capped at two exercises. Unilateral exercises: 1 of 20 asks for left and right logged separately (Antonio). Paywall: 1 of 20 says the free tier stops at 4 workouts and 7 exercises (Jason Douglas, Aug 2025). Health sync: 1 of 20 reports workouts not reaching Samsung Health through Health Connect (John Mungai, Mar 2025). Keyboard: 1 of 20 says they will cancel over a keyboard change in a recent update (Michael Fowler, Nov 2026); the change itself is not identified. Community feed: 1 of 20 complains about inappropriate posts in the social feed (Antonio). Bugs in general: 1 of 20 says no crashes or bugs (T).

AI and planning: no review mentions an AI planner directly. The listing's "What's new" says "Connect Hevy to Claude from Settings > Integrations" and that "Hevy Trainer now uses 12-20 reps for hard-to-progress exercises." One reviewer says the app pushes sets to failure and scales back (Az Lev-Har). That is 1 of 20 and is about auto-progression.

Quote (one only): Michael Fowler, Nov 2026: "Way more efficient and useful than a notebook or text doc on your phone."

Listing claims not checked on a device: Wear OS live sync, heart rate during Wear OS workouts, Wear OS tile.

## Strong (io.strongapp.strong)

Listing: 4.3 stars, 42.8K reviews (dialog header shows 42.4K), 1M+ downloads, updated Nov 17, 2026, rated Everyone, in-app purchases. Developer replies in the sample: 5, all on reviews from 2019 to 2021, most pointing to support@strongapp.io.

About half the sample is from 2018 to 2021. About 10 of 20 are from Aug to Nov 2026.

Praise: about 4 of 20 praise simplicity, clean interface or easy number entry during a workout (one 2019 review says "easy to input numbers as I'm working out"; a Sept 2026 review says "easy on the eyes, clear, functional, intuitive"). About 3 of 20 praise progress and personal-best tracking. One 2026 review praises the new watch app as making it "even more easy to use" (platform not stated in the review). About 2 of 20 praise the support team (one 2021 review, and a developer reply). About 3 of 20 say they have used it for 7 to 10 years.

Complaints: pricing and paywall in 4 of 20. A 2018 reviewer says lifetime is about $100 and wants ads or in-app purchases instead. A 2026 reviewer says pro features are overpriced. A 2021 reviewer says a one-time premium purchase was lost and had to be bought again on subscription. A 2020 reviewer says the free tier is limited to three routines. Account and sign-in friction in 2 of 20: a 2018 reviewer cannot log in, and a 2026 reviewer says an account is required before seeing what the app offers. Exercise library in 3 of 20: a 2026 reviewer says the library needs an overhaul (Ryan Nunwick), one says there is no triceps overhead and muscle grouping is coarse (Rafael Lima), and one says kettlebells are missing (Russ Kimzey). Timers in 3 of 20 are about non-rest timers: a tabata timer, an AMRAP timer, a HIIT mode, and a per-exercise exercise timer removed in a 2019 version. None of the 20 complains about the rest timer. Bugs in 2 of 20 from Aug 2026: the back button minimises an open workout on Android (Chris Atik, 11 helpful votes), and editing an exercise during a workout does not update the saved template (Shafaat Khan). The same template complaint is a 2026 praise point in another review, which says the app asks whether to update the template or only the values. Data: 2 of 20 ask for backups or for a working export and import.

Demo media: 1 of 20 says a demo image shows a bad bench press position (2019).

AI: no review mentions AI features. One 2026 reviewer says an AI tool could rebuild the app quickly (Luis Niebla).

Quote (one only): Panayiotis Motis, Sept 2026: "Easy on the eyes, clear, functional, intuitive."

## Fitbod (com.fitbod.fitbod)

Listing: 4.5 stars, 32.2K reviews (dialog header shows 31.4K), 1M+ downloads, updated Nov 23, 2026, rated Everyone, in-app purchases. Data safety on the listing says financial info is shared with third parties; this was not checked further. Developer replies: about 17 of the 19 reviews in the sample, most pointing to support@fitbod.me.

Praise: about 4 of 19 praise the AI plan as a personal trainer or say it adapts (for example one reviewer says it is "painless" and the suggested weights are useful). About 3 of 19 praise ease of use. Two praise the how-to videos. One praises the plate calculator and timer. One reviewer lost 77 lb with it.

Plan quality: about 6 of 19 complain about the generated plan. One says the plan "does what it wants" and brings back an exercise a few days after it was removed (J O'Br). One says a 45-minute setting produced only one or two exercises (a Nov 2026 review). One says there is no progressive overload: a logged 800 lb press history produced a plan for 590 lb (Adam Krawczyk, Sept 2026). One says the plan gets stuck on the same exercises (Robert Taylor). One says the plan ignores a limited kettlebell count. One wants weight stacks per machine. The developer replies point to the My Plan settings and to "don't recommend again".

Paywall and price in about 6 of 19. A monthly price of $15.99 is named twice. One review says the app shows a subscription screen before a workout can be created (Fercho Serna, Sept 2026). One says free-tier content is blocked (K Hogarth). One says records cannot be reached after the subscription lapses (Hanosz Prime). One says a card screen after sign-up blocks account deletion (Evgeny Dimitrov, Oct 2026). The developer says there are no ads, when one reviewer says there are ads (Ben W).

Bugs: about 4 of 19 call the app buggy or malfunctioning.

Exercise media: AI-generated demo videos are called incorrect in one review (J O'Br). Other reviews praise the how-to access.

Timer: one wants the rest timer to respect limited workout windows and fatigue across muscle groups (David Mogler). Another praises the timer and plate calculator (Lisa Monk). One asks for a heart-rate monitor pairing answer (Lisa Monk).

Quote (one only): J O'Br, Nov 2026: "it does what it wants, adds sets reps, and exercises as it feels like."

## What this means for Delt Arena

Live set screen. Reviews of all three apps are mostly about what the plan does and what the paywall blocks. Logging speed itself is rarely the subject. The reviews that matter for the live screen are about state consistency. Strong users get bitten when an in-workout edit does not reach the routine, and the best-rated handling in the sample is a prompt asking whether to update the template or only the values. Strong also loses work when the back button minimises an open workout. The lesson for the live set screen is to keep an in-progress workout stable across navigation and to ask before writing an edit back to the template. This is inferred from 2 to 3 reviews per app and should be checked with a larger sample.

Rest timer. Hevy users ask for three specific things: per-set rest durations, a timer that starts after the whole exercise, and a timer visible outside the app. Fitbod users ask for a rest timer that respects a limited window. Strong users in this sample do not complain about the rest timer, but ask for other interval timers. The rest timer should allow a per-set override, a "rest after exercise" option, and a visible running state. Keep these as candidates; they come from 1 to 3 reviews each.

One-hand tap logging. No reviewer in any sample describes one-handed use, tap counts or thumb reach, so this cannot be evidenced from these reviews. Two related points do come up: unilateral exercises need separate left and right logging (Hevy, 1 review, and Fitbod, 1 review asking for one-limb training), and superset limits and set structures need flexibility (Hevy, 1 review). Any claim about one-hand logging needs its own research.

Paywall. All three apps gate features behind subscriptions, and the paywall complaints cluster around pricing and losing access to past data. Delt Arena should make sure past logs are never locked behind a subscription, since two of the three apps draw complaints for exactly that.

AI planning. Fitbod's planner draws the most complaints in the sample, mainly about progression logic and ignoring equipment and time limits. Hevy has the lightest AI footprint and draws praise for custom routines. Strong has none. The lesson is that any generated plan needs visible reasons for each change and respect for equipment and time constraints.

Wear OS. Only Hevy has a Wear OS app discussed in these reviews. Fitbod and Strong have no Wear OS praise or complaints in the sample.

## Still not reached

The "Most helpful" sort was not used. The dialog default is "Most relevant", so helpfulness order is not fully reflected.

Star-filtered samples (1 and 2 star) were not pulled for any app. Those are where bugs and paywall complaints are most concentrated.

Sample size is about 20 per app. Counts are approximate hand tallies and should be treated as directional.

Only the US storefront was read. Reviews in other regions were not checked.

Hevy: "Connect Hevy to Claude" and the "Hevy Trainer" change are listing text, not reviews, and were not tested. The Samsung Health sync report is from 2025 and may be fixed.

Strong: the watch app platform was not confirmed, and the watch app was not tested. Backup and cloud sync are listed, but the reviews do not confirm they work.

Fitbod: the "ads" claim and the data safety listing (financial info shared with third parties) were not checked further.

Nothing was tested on a device. Every statement here is from the listing and review text only.
